# Exam prep — what actually gets asked

Grouped by unit. The repeaters are starred in my head; numericals always come.

## Unit 1 — DC fundamentals
- Ohm's law and its limitations (4 marks)
- Derive equivalent R for series and parallel; two-resistor parallel formula (6 marks)
- State KVL and KCL (4 marks)
- Star–delta (Y–Δ) transformation, derive the relations (8 marks)
- Effect of temperature on resistance, define α (5 marks)
- Numerical: network reduction / bridge, find total current (8 marks)

## Unit 2 — Network theorems
- State and prove Thevenin's theorem (8 marks)
- Superposition theorem, two-source numerical (8 marks)
- Norton's theorem and its relation to Thevenin (6 marks)
- Maximum power transfer theorem, derive $R_L=R_{th}$, $P_{max}=V_{th}^2/4R_{th}$ (6 marks)
- Numerical: current in a load branch by Thevenin (10 marks)

## Unit 3 — AC fundamentals
- Derive RMS and average value of a sinusoid (8 marks)
- Define form factor and peak factor, values for a sine (4 marks)
- Advantages of AC over DC (4 marks)
- Phasor representation of an alternating quantity (5 marks)

## Unit 4 — Single-phase AC
- Active, reactive, apparent power and power factor; the power triangle (6 marks)
- Series R–L–C: derive Z and phase angle, phasor diagram (8 marks)
- Series resonance: derive $f_r$, Q-factor, bandwidth (10 marks)
- Power factor — meaning, why low p.f. is bad, how to improve it (5 marks)
- Numerical: R–L or R–L–C series, find I, p.f., power (8 marks)

## Unit 5 — Three-phase systems
- Line and phase relations in star and delta, derive (8 marks)
- Power in a balanced 3-phase load, $P=\sqrt3 V_L I_L\cos\phi$ (6 marks)
- Two-wattmeter method: power and p.f. from $W_1,W_2$ (10 marks)
- Advantages of three-phase over single-phase (4 marks)

## Unit 6 — Transformers
- Derive the EMF equation $E=4.44 f N\Phi_m$ (8 marks)
- Construction and working principle (6 marks)
- Losses; condition for maximum efficiency ($P_{cu}=P_i$) (8 marks)
- OC and SC tests — what each gives (8 marks)
- Define voltage regulation (4 marks)
- Numerical: efficiency and/or regulation (8 marks)

## Unit 7 — DC machines
- Derive the EMF equation of a DC generator (8 marks)
- Working of a DC motor, significance of back EMF (6 marks)
- Torque equation, $T\propto\Phi I_a$ (6 marks)
- Types of generators/motors (shunt, series, compound) (6 marks)
- Numerical: back EMF, speed, or torque (8 marks)

## Unit 8 — AC machines
- Production of a rotating magnetic field (6 marks)
- Principle of a 3-phase induction motor; define slip (8 marks)
- Derive rotor frequency $f_r=sf$ (5 marks)
- Why a single-phase induction motor is not self-starting (6 marks)
- Torque–slip characteristic, max torque at $s=R_2/X_2$ (8 marks)
- Numerical: synchronous speed and slip (6 marks)

## Unit 9 — Measuring instruments
- The three torques in an indicating instrument (6 marks)
- PMMC construction and working; why DC only (8 marks)
- Compare PMMC and moving iron (6 marks)
- Extend range of ammeter (shunt) and voltmeter (multiplier), derive (6 marks)
- Dynamometer wattmeter — construction and working (8 marks)
- Single-phase induction energy meter (8 marks)
- Numerical: shunt / multiplier (6 marks)

## Unit 10 — Wiring and safety
- Types of domestic wiring — cleat, casing-capping, conduit (6 marks)
- Two-way and staircase wiring with diagram (6 marks)
- Fluorescent tube circuit — role of choke and starter (8 marks)
- Fuse vs MCB; fuse rating and fusing factor (5 marks)
- Working of ELCB / RCCB (6 marks)
- Necessity of earthing; pipe vs plate earthing (8 marks)
- Why the earth pin is longer and thicker (4 marks)
- Numerical: monthly energy bill / fuse rating (6 marks)
